# COSMIC_ENGINE_PHILOSOPHY.md — The North Star

This is not a technical document. It is the artistic contract every future pass is measured against.
Roadmap phases and pass briefs should cite it. When a technical decision and this document disagree,
stop and resolve the disagreement explicitly — do not quietly ship the technical convenience.

---

## What Cosmic Engine is

Cosmic Engine is a **live cinematic instrument**. It is played, not watched. It belongs on stage behind a
psych/prog rock band, where it must hold an audience's peripheral attention for a whole song and reward the
moments they look directly at it — without ever competing with the band for the center of the experience.

It is not a music visualizer. A visualizer decorates a signal. Cosmic Engine tells a slow story that the
music conducts.

## What makes a visual feel like Cosmic Engine

Every accepted scene in this project's history shares the same DNA, learned pass by pass:

- **Dark-dominant, always.** Light is precious. The frame earns its bright moments by spending most of its
  time in shadow. A scene that washes out — orange, blue, or rainbow — has failed, no matter how impressive
  the technique. Darkness is what makes the audience lean in.
- **Slow transformation over song-length time.** The unit of drama is the *arc*, not the frame: calm →
  awakening → build → peak → afterglow. Nothing important happens instantly. A viewer who looks up twice,
  minutes apart, should see a different world and understand that something has been happening while they
  looked away.
- **Sensed more than seen.** The best elements in this engine's history are the ones at the edge of
  perception: a vast shadow that might be moving out in the dark, a fire that starts as a glow on the
  horizon, an abyss that turns out to be full of stars. Implication over depiction. Negative space over
  detail. If it can be fully seen, it is probably too literal.
- **Organic, never mechanical.** This project has re-learned the same lesson repeatedly: evenly spaced
  copies, rigid pivoting shapes, uniform particles, and single-frequency motion all read as *machinery*.
  Irregularity, independent phase, traveling waves, and asymmetric envelopes (fast attack, slow release —
  swell and linger) read as *life*. Everything that moves should move like it is suspended in a medium.
- **Restraint as a style.** The rejected experiments (ornamental highlights, decorative detail, extra
  brightness "to make it pop") failed because decoration is not intensity. Intensity comes from scale,
  darkness, patience, and payoff.

## The purpose of video atoms

Procedural rendering is this engine's soul, but it has a known ceiling: it does not produce *things* —
a burning turbine, a beating heart, a crowd, a wolf — without either enormous effort or cartoon results.
Video atoms exist to supply **the real**: short, curated fragments of cinematography that carry immediate,
concrete meaning.

The division of labor is strict:
- **Footage supplies recognition** — the subject, the gravity, the documentary weight.
- **The engine supplies breath** — atmosphere, motion, color, reactivity, transitions, and the slow arc
  that footage alone cannot sustain.

A video atom is never a backdrop with effects on top, and procedural work is never wallpaper behind a clip.
The target is a *third thing*: footage seen as if through this engine's water, smoke, and light — enhanced,
interrupted, dissolved, and reborn by the procedural layer. If the composite ever looks like a media player
with a screensaver overlaid, the pass has failed.

## The role of volumetric and procedural rendering

Procedural scenes remain first-class citizens forever — some songs want pure abstraction. Within hybrid
scenes, the procedural layer is the **connective tissue**: it is what a clip emerges from and returns to,
what reacts to the band in real time, what makes two unrelated fragments of footage feel like one dream.
Volumetric atmosphere (fog, god rays, depth, particulate) is the primary tool that welds footage into the
frame instead of leaving it pasted on.

## Controlled randomness, not chaos

No two performances should be identical; every performance must be *intentional*. The engine's answer is
seeded randomness: variation between shows, reproducibility within one (a seed can recreate any show for
review). Randomness chooses **among curated options** — which clip, which drift path, which moment the
shadow passes — never the aesthetics themselves. Chaos is a failure mode: motion that destroys the hypnotic
mood, transitions without narrative logic, elements fighting for attention. When in doubt, less, slower,
darker.

## Recurring motifs

The band's visual language draws from a consistent well. These motifs recur, rhyme, and transform into one
another across scenes and songs:

- **Cosmos** — stars, nebulae, dust; the vast and indifferent.
- **Water and the deep** — oceans, bioluminescence, pressure, what lives where light doesn't reach.
- **Fire and industry** — turbines, flame, smoke, machines against a burning sky; beauty in destruction.
- **The body** — heartbeat, blood, breath; the human scale inside the cosmic one.
- **The living world** — forests, storms, wildlife; growth and weather as slow music.
- **Humanity in numbers** — crowds, ritual, dance, unrest; graveyards and memory.

The most Cosmic Engine moments are the *transitions between* motifs: plankton becoming stars, veins becoming
rivers, a crowd becoming a current. Any new scene or atom should know which motif it serves and which motif
it could dissolve into.

## What to avoid

- Cheap effects and gimmicks: lens flares, strobe abuse, stock "trippy" filters, EQ-bar energy.
- Random transitions: every cut or dissolve must have a reason in the music or the arc.
- Excessive filtering: grading serves mood; stacking effects until footage is unrecognizable is noise.
- Literalism and cartoons: no drawn monsters, no mascot creatures, no clip-art symbolism.
- Center-parked composition: nothing sits still in the middle of frame for a whole song.
- Brightness as a crutch: if a layer is invisible, fix its integration, not its exposure.
- Novelty for its own sake: a new technique earns its place only when it serves the arc.

## The long-term goal

An instrument the band can *play*: pick a mood, trust the engine to perform a unique, seeded, song-length
cinematic piece that listens to them — and have every show feel composed, not generated. The measure of
success is not technical: it is that someone in the audience, days later, describes something they saw as
if it had been made deliberately, for that song, for that night. It was.
